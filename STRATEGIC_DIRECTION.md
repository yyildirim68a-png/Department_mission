# Strategic direction: building a distinctive research identity

**Discussion draft for the next 10–15 years | November 23, 2026**

## Strategic goal

Build a research group that finance and economics scholars recognize for explaining **how the flow of capital between property-level markets and securities markets affects real estate investment, liquidity, valuation, and risk**.

The department should preserve its existing strengths in mortgage and housing economics while becoming deeper in **commercial real estate financing, institutional investment, and asset pricing**.

## Present assets

1. Top-tier peer-reviewed publications in general finance and economics by multiple current faculty.
2. Research on property transactions, brokers, housing search, mortgage origination, MBS, CMBS, contracts, property rights, institutional investors, and valuation.
3. Complementary methods spanning empirical finance, economic theory, asset pricing, and quantitative modeling.
4. New York City location and a business-school setting that may support research partnerships and access to institutional questions and data, subject to data rights and compliance.

## Areas where the next research hire could add distinctive depth

- **Commercial real estate debt and credit:** origination, refinancing, bank exposure, CMBS, defaults, and restructuring.
- **Commercial property asset pricing:** cash flows, cap rates, discount rates, rental contracts, risk premia, and returns.
- **Institutional real estate capital:** REITs, private real estate funds, portfolio allocation, and real estate equity/debt financing.
- **Property-to-securities transmission:** how occupancy, operating income, contracts and property shocks are reflected in mortgage and bond prices.

These are strategic gaps to deepen, not claims that the department currently has no work in those areas.

## Hiring principle

**Prioritize independent, repeatable top-journal research ability within a coherent financial-economics research direction, while using the appointment to add expertise that is not already widely represented.**

Evidence to scrutinize:

- Importance and originality of the candidate's research questions to finance/economics beyond the real estate setting.
- Credibility of identification or theoretical/modeling assumptions.
- Independent intellectual contribution to coauthored papers.
- Quality and feasibility of the next three or four projects, not only the job market paper.
- Realistic trajectory to sustained publication at leading finance, economics, and real estate journals.
- Ability to contribute to real estate finance and capital-markets teaching, without treating teaching fit as a substitute for research quality.

A provisional decision rubric previously discussed for this search is: 40% long-term top-journal potential, 30% research-identity contribution, 20% complementarity, and 10% teaching/institutional contribution. **These are proposed discussion weights, not adopted policy or a mechanical prediction of career outcomes.**

## Build reputation through activity, not slogans

1. **A recurring research seminar or workshop:** emphasize the financing–market structure–valuation chain rather than grouping talks only by property type.
2. **Coherent research pages:** present representative faculty papers by mechanism, with publication status and paper links.
3. **Cross-field collaboration:** encourage work bridging property cash flows and finance, housing search and market microstructure, and institutional ownership and asset pricing.
4. **Distinguishing scholarly outputs:** target repeated publications with contributions to finance and economics; do not count R&Rs as published.
5. **Research partnerships:** where feasible, develop research questions and data partnerships with real estate financiers and investors, under clear confidentiality arrangements.

## A useful 2035–2040 test

Would an external finance professor describe Baruch as a group whose papers help explain **how real estate is financed, how markets for property and mortgage securities function, and how real estate assets are valued**? If so, the research identity has become credible through scholarship rather than branding.
