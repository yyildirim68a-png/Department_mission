# Three linked research pillars

**Working interpretation of existing faculty research—not individual performance ratings.**

## 1. How is real estate financed and owned?

Research mechanisms: mortgage origination, credit access, refinancing, institutional ownership, securitization, funding and investment constraints, and contractual risk sharing.

**Examples of relevant faculty research:** Yildirim (CMBS, commercial mortgage risk, leases and debt); Li (MBS issuance and market structure); Hu (property rights, real estate presales, REIT organization); Coven (institutional investors, financing constraints); Yoon (origination and refinancing frictions). Gilbukh also connects through FHA/VA financing and access to housing transactions.

## 2. How do real estate and financial markets function?

Research mechanisms: brokerage, search, information asymmetry, intermediary incentives, OTC securities trading, segmentation, transaction costs, and market liquidity.

**Examples:** Gilbukh (agents, search, transactions); Li (dealer expertise, MBS and OTC trading); Hu (housing search, speculation and bidding); Yoon (nonbank intermediation); Coven (institutional investor allocation); Yildirim (CMBS and information, liquidity and credit risk).

## 3. How are real estate assets priced and allocated?

Research mechanisms: asset pricing, property cash flows, leases, property rights, default risk, rental markets, returns, and decisions to own, rent, finance, or invest.

**Examples:** Yildirim (lease valuation, CMBS, REITs, illiquid asset pricing); Hu (property-rights valuation, presale pricing, housing market dynamics); Coven (ownership and allocation); Li (MBS asset pricing); Gilbukh (transaction pricing and price-to-rent signals); Yoon (mortgage costs and landlord distress).

## Key insight

The department's most credible common identity is not simply **housing economics** or **commercial real estate finance**. It is the study of **how financing and market institutions determine the allocation and value of real estate assets**. Commercial real estate capital markets offer a particularly attractive area to expand—not an already-uniform focus across the faculty.
