# Proposed research mission

**Status: Discussion draft, not officially approved.**  
**Date: November 23, 2026**

## Research identity

**The Financial Economics of Real Estate**

**Distinctive specialization:** *Capital, Intermediation, and Asset Valuation*  
**Positioning line:** *From Property Transactions to Capital Markets*

## Recommended public-facing statement

The William Newman Department of Real Estate at Baruch College advances the financial economics of real estate, with a particular focus on **capital, intermediation, and asset valuation**.

Our faculty investigate how financial institutions, investors, intermediaries, contracts, and market frictions shape the financing, trading, pricing, and allocation of real estate assets. Our research spans property transactions, mortgage origination, institutional investment, housing markets, commercial real estate, and mortgage-backed securities.

By connecting property-level markets with financial capital markets, we address fundamental questions in asset pricing, financial intermediation, and investment. Our faculty contribute to leading finance, economics, and real estate journals and use real estate institutions and data to illuminate broader financial-economic mechanisms.

## Short form (for brochure, website header, and presentations)

**We study how capital, financial institutions, and market frictions determine real estate financing, transactions, investment, and asset values—from individual properties to mortgage securities and capital markets.**

## Research proposition

Real estate is a highly intermediated and often illiquid asset market. Contracts, financing arrangements, information, trading institutions, and investment constraints matter for economic outcomes. The department studies these mechanisms across property and securities markets using empirical, quantitative, and theoretical approaches.

## What this does—and does not—claim

- **Present strength:** A substantial concentration in housing and mortgage finance, market intermediation, contracts, financial frictions, and asset valuation.
- **Present strength:** Peer-reviewed work across leading finance, economics, and real estate outlets.
- **Opportunity:** A broader multi-faculty cluster in commercial real estate capital markets, institutional investment, commercial lending, and property cash-flow pricing.
- **Not a claim:** Every faculty member works on the same market, or that commercial property investment is already the department's dominant specialization.
- **Not a claim:** This intellectual theme is unique to Baruch; distinctiveness must be earned through repeated scholarly contributions and the connection from property transactions to capital markets.

## Proposed external narrative

Baruch's distinctive contribution is to link **property-level market mechanisms** (search, brokerage, ownership, credit access, contracts, leases, and rents) to **financial-market mechanisms** (intermediation, securitization, liquidity, risk pricing, and capital allocation). This connection spans residential and commercial real estate without treating either as a catch-all label.
