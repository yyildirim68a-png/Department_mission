# Prompt: Plain-Language Summaries of Faculty Research for Industry

Oct 30, 2026 · @Eunkyu

## What I need

This folder holds, for each of our faculty members, research papers they have published in academic journals. **Please turn them into one PDF of plain-language summaries: one summary per paper, 1–2 pages each, grouped by faculty member.**

The summaries will be shared with finance and real estate professionals. They will also feed our research newsletter, which highlights recent faculty research for industry readers and the general public. Together, they should show what our faculty have studied and what that work contributes to the industry.

## The key point

Every summary should answer the question an industry reader actually has: **"What does this research mean for my market, my deals, or my decisions?"** That makes **Implications and Market Insights** the most important section of each summary, so give it the most space and the most care.

## Audience and tone

- **Readers:** finance and real estate finance professionals with limited background in quantitative methods. Newsletter readers also include the general public.
- **Voice:** write like a sharp industry research note, not an academic paper. Use plain language and the industry's own vocabulary where it fits (pricing, risk, returns, cap rates, underwriting, affordability).
- **No equations or jargon:** leave out model specifications, regression tables, and econometric terms. If a technical idea is essential, explain it in one plain sentence.
- **Results in practical terms:** describe each result by its direction and practical size (e.g., "rents were about 5% higher"), not by coefficients or significance levels. Spell out acronyms on first use.

## What each summary includes

**Header**

- Title
- Journal
- Year published
- Authors
- **Key takeaway:** one or two sentences, in bold, stating the main insight for industry

**Body: use these five sections, in this order**

1. **Introduction:** why the study matters, covering its motivation, background, and market or policy context.
2. **Research Questions:** the main questions the study sets out to answer (usually one to three), stated plainly.
3. **Data and Approach:** a short, non-technical account of how the study was done (the data, the setting and time period, and the basic approach).
4. **Main Findings:** the key results (typically three to five), in plain terms.
5. **Implications and Market Insights** *(most important; roughly a third of the summary)*: who should care (investors, lenders, developers, policymakers, and so on), what the findings mean for their decisions, and the risks or opportunities they point to.

**Reference:** close each summary with the full citation in APA 6th edition style, including the DOI if available.

**Length:** 1–2 pages per paper, never more than 2 (roughly 500–850 words).

## How to organize the PDF

- Open with a contents page listing each faculty member and their papers.
- Group the summaries by faculty member, with each person's name as the section heading. Open each section with a two- to three-sentence snapshot of that person's research focus and its relevance to industry.
- Within each section, order papers from most recent to oldest. Start each summary on a new page so it can go straight into the newsletter.
- If two or more of our faculty members co-authored a paper, summarize it once and cross-reference it in the other faculty members' sections.

## Accuracy

- Stay faithful to each paper: no numbers or claims it doesn't contain, and no implications its findings don't support.
- Don't describe a relationship as cause and effect unless the authors do.
- Take the title, journal, year, authors, and DOI from the paper itself. If anything is missing or unclear, write **[verify]** instead of guessing.
- Before finishing, check each summary against its paper.

## Deliverable

A single PDF with clean, consistent formatting, ready to share with an outside audience. In your reply, list anything you marked [verify] so I can check it before the PDF goes out.
